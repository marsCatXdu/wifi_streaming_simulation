# Paired temporal-T2 qualification against STR MLO

This directory is the compact, version-controlled snapshot of the first
48-pair closed-loop engineering qualification of the frozen primary-only
temporal-T2 selective-duplication policy.  It is valid negative evidence: the
candidate passed both resource targets but did not establish either required
performance victory against STR MLO.

The experiment used the unchanged neutral mixed-4x4 environment, seeds
`1201` through `1248`, run `1`, and one policy/STR pair per seed.  Every one of
the 96 raw runs passed the manifest-pinned strict validator.  Reserved final
confirmation seeds `1301` through `1348` were not used.

## Qualification result

| Metric | Temporal-T2 | STR MLO | Paired comparison | Gate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| All-generated deadline-miss rate | 0.7882% (681/86,400) | 0.7049% (609/86,400) | policy-minus-STR 95% interval `[-0.0567, +0.2269]` percentage points | Fail |
| Mean per-run completed-frame HF7 P99 | 17.416 ms | 18.113 ms | policy-minus-STR 95% interval `[-1.816, +0.310]` ms | Fail |
| Sender PHY airtime ratio | - | - | 1.1324, 95% interval `[1.0963, 1.1660]` | Pass (`< 1.20`) |
| Background-throughput loss | - | - | 0.0014%, 95% interval `[-0.0017%, 0.0048%]` | Pass (`<= 1%`) |

The P99 point estimate is favorable by 0.697 ms, but its interval crosses
zero.  The miss-rate point estimate is worse by 0.0833 percentage points and
also is not decisive.  Therefore the strict overall status is **fail**.  This
campaign is engineering qualification only, not final confirmation.

Completed-frame latency always appears beside the all-generated miss rate.
Late completions remain in the P99 population; incomplete frames remain in
the miss denominator.  The standard completion CDF/PDF figures are descriptive
and must not replace the paired per-run qualification estimand.

## Admission diagnosis

Duplication itself is effective.  Reconstructing the primary-copy outcome in
the policy runs gives 1,219 misses and a 19.604 ms mean per-run P99.  The
launched secondary copies rescue 538 deadline misses and reduce those values
to 681 misses and 17.416 ms.

Of the 4,953 actions, 3,144 accelerate a primary copy that already meets the
deadline, 1,258 give no completion-time benefit, 538 rescue a primary miss,
and 13 remain late or incomplete.  The no-benefit population consumes 34.34%
of measured action airtime, so better treated-outcome and secondary-cost
prediction remains worthwhile after admission is repaired.

| Decision status | Frames | Primary-copy misses | Union misses |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Action | 4,953 | 551 | 13 |
| Airtime guard rejected | 2,276 | 349 | 349 |
| Below score threshold | 34,928 | 162 | 162 |
| History warmup | 384 | 83 | 83 |
| I-frame restricted | 1,392 | 64 | 64 |
| Outside decision window | 768 | 10 | 10 |
| Not actionable | 41,699 | 0 | 0 |

The current primary predictor is not the main failure.  On the 42,157
evaluated frames, its exploratory deadline-miss ranking AUC is 0.922 and its
bad12 AUC is 0.875.  Mean predicted primary bad12 risk is 12.17% versus 11.51%
observed.  On launched frames, predicted treated bad12 risk is 2.97% versus
3.03% observed.

The measured-airtime guard is the bottleneck.  Guard-rejected candidates have
a 15.33% primary-copy miss rate, compared with 11.12% among admitted actions,
and have a higher mean model score.  The chronological token bucket spends
credit on earlier lower-ranked candidates and later rejects riskier ones.  The
2,276 rejected candidates contain 349 misses, more than half of the policy's
681 final misses.

The controller used 231.9 ms of measured secondary airtime per run.  The
rejected rows carry 109.4 ms/run of predicted additional cost, putting all
score passers at roughly 341.3 ms/run, below the 360 ms/run generated by a
0.6% long-run refill over 60 seconds.  A static one-for-one projection gives a
sender-airtime ratio near 1.159.  This is strong evidence of burst-capacity
clipping rather than exhaustion of the average <1.20 target, but only a new
closed-loop run can validate the projected cost and induced contention.

A noncausal, full-run same-count ranking diagnostic selects the top 4,953
current-model scores separately within each run and covers 749 primary misses,
versus 551 under chronological admission.  This is only a lookahead upper
bound, not a deployable result, but it demonstrates allocation headroom with
the existing predictor and action volume.  Admission/rationing should be fixed
before replacing the model.  Cold-start and I-frame handling remain secondary
opportunities because they account for 83 and 64 misses, respectively.

## Why this is worse than the primary-tail T4 campaign

The closest old arm is `full_copy_cost_0p0055`; the old
`full_copy_cost_0p0080` arm is also useful as its more aggressive balanced
operating point:

| Quantity | Current temporal-T2 | Old T4 0.0055 | Old T4 0.0080 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Matched development pairs | 48 | 12 | 12 |
| Nominal measured-airtime budget | 0.6% | 2.0% | 2.0% |
| Actions / all generated frames | 5.73% | 8.06% | 11.15% |
| Measured secondary airtime / run | 231.9 ms | 378.8 ms | 537.8 ms |
| Policy miss rate | 0.7882% | 0.3056% | 0.2130% |
| STR miss rate | 0.7049% | 0.7593% | 0.7593% |
| Policy completed P99 | 17.416 ms | 15.141 ms | 13.979 ms |
| Sender-airtime ratio versus STR | 1.132 | 1.152 | 1.190 |

The old controller covered far more of its policy run's observable primary
misses.  The current policy acts on 551/1,219 (45.2%) and rescues 538/551
(97.6%).  The old 0.0055 arm acts on 290/340 (85.3%) and rescues 274/290
(94.5%); the old 0.0080 arm covers 310/340 (91.2%) and rescues 294/310
(94.8%).  Duplication therefore works at least as reliably now, but the
current controller admits too few risky frames.

The supported causes are the 0.6% burst token bucket, chronological admission,
the exact-lag history warmup, and the P-frame-only gate.  The current guard
rejects 31.5% of score passers, whereas the old 0.0055 arm records only four
bucket deferrals.  Warmup and excluded I frames contribute another 147 current
misses.  The old T4 predictor also observes two additional milliseconds and
has an I-frame T0 stage; those differences may help but are not isolated here.
The current live score still recalls 84.75% of evaluated primary misses before
the guard, so the data do not support blaming the primary-risk predictor
first.

This is not an environment regression.  Both campaigns declare the same STR
resolved configuration, mixed-4x4 environment, ns-3 upstream revision, and
compiler/build mode.  A seed-43 STR control reproduced its summary exactly
between builds.  Current-campaign STR is slightly easier in aggregate (0.7049%
and 18.113 ms versus 0.7593% and 18.664 ms), which is far too small to explain
the treatment gap.

The cross-campaign treatment comparison is nevertheless not causal: the old
result uses only 12 post-selection development seeds `43` through `54`, while
the current result uses 48 different seeds.  Running the old 0.0055/0.0080 T4
arms on the current engineering seeds is the clean mechanism control.  Also,
the old 0.0055 arm's sender-airtime point ratio was below 1.20, but its upper
confidence endpoint was 1.220, so that campaign's own stricter overall gate
still reported `FAIL`.

## Seed heterogeneity

The candidate wins the miss comparison in 21 pairs, ties in 7, and loses in
20; it wins the P99 comparison in 25 and loses in 23.  The miss point direction
remains worse in every leave-one-out sample, so it is not caused by one seed.

Seeds `1241`, `1245`, and `1248` produce unusually large joint wins and pull
the P99 mean downward, despite launching only 18, 16, and 5 copies.  Their
gains therefore mostly reflect matched topology/regime variation rather than
the intervention.  Conversely, seeds `1231`, `1213`, `1204`, and `1222`
contribute 87 excess policy misses.  High guard-rejection runs are the stable
failure regime.

The standard burst plot adds an important qualification.  Temporal-T2 and STR
have nearly the same number of miss episodes (498 versus 502), and temporal-T2
has the smaller maximum burst (10 versus 14), but temporal-T2 has 106
multi-frame episodes versus 57 for STR.  Those episodes contain 289 versus 164
missed frames.  The candidate therefore does not meet the absolute-reliability
goal by trading a few long bursts for many shorter ones; this pattern is
consistent with credit exhaustion during difficult periods.
